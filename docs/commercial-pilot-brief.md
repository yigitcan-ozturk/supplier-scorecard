# Commercial Pilot Brief

Status: **Phase 3 working brief**  
Issue: #22

## Positioning

Supplier Scorecard is **explainable procurement decision infrastructure for engineering procurement**.

It combines independently inspectable commercial, payment, supplier-risk and technical-compliance evidence into a deterministic supplier decision, applies explicit procurement policy, and preserves a tamper-evident audit record for human review.

It is not an opaque AI supplier-ranking system and it does not automate contractual award.

## Best-fit buyer

Initial target profile:

- industrial manufacturers and engineering-led buyers;
- procurement / strategic sourcing teams evaluating technically differentiated bids;
- categories where lowest quoted price is not sufficient to select safely;
- recurring supplier-quality, compliance, payment-exposure or technical-deviation concerns;
- workflows still dependent on email, spreadsheets and manually assembled decision packs.

## Proposed paid-pilot shape

A bounded pilot should focus on one procurement category or a limited number of live RFQs.

### Included

- supplier quotation/evidence ingestion;
- currency and quotation normalization when required;
- payment-exposure interpretation;
- current vendor-risk and optional historical-trend evidence;
- technical-compliance signal when available;
- policy-aware supplier ranking;
- explicit REVIEW/BLOCKED handling;
- tamper-evident decision records;
- pilot findings report with measured operational and decision-quality observations.

### Pilot success measures

- reduction in manual preparation effort;
- reduction in decision lead time;
- fewer manual spreadsheet/touch points;
- useful risk/policy findings surfaced;
- decision reproducibility and auditability;
- reviewer confidence in explanation quality.

## Buyer discovery questions

1. How many supplier evaluations/RFQs does the team complete per month?
2. How many suppliers are typically compared per RFQ?
3. How much analyst/buyer time is spent preparing one recommendation?
4. Which factors besides quoted price drive the final award decision?
5. Where do supplier-risk, payment-term and technical-deviation checks happen today?
6. Which decisions require management, compliance or engineering review?
7. How is the final recommendation justified or audited later?
8. Which errors or omissions are most expensive?
9. Would deployment need to be hosted, private-cloud or local/on-premise?
10. What measurable outcome would justify paying for a pilot?

## Qualification indicators

Strong fit if several are true:

- 3+ suppliers frequently compared per technical RFQ;
- manual spreadsheets are the primary decision layer;
- multiple departments contribute evidence;
- high-value / engineered / regulated / quality-sensitive categories;
- repeated manual effort assembling award recommendations;
- buyer must explain decisions to management/audit/engineering;
- data arrives from heterogeneous tools and formats.

Weak fit if:

- purchases are simple commodity reorders;
- supplier choice is almost always lowest price only;
- there is negligible supplier-risk or technical differentiation;
- procurement volume does not justify workflow tooling.

## Commercial hypotheses to validate

Do not lock pricing before pilot evidence. Test:

1. **Fixed-scope paid pilot** — limited RFQs/category, defined success metrics.
2. **Team subscription** — based on procurement volume / user team.
3. **Enterprise/private deployment** — annual license plus security/integration requirements.
4. **Implementation/integration fee** — for ERP/source-system connectors and policy configuration.

## Evidence required before final pricing

- buyer hours saved per RFQ;
- annual RFQ volume;
- risk/quality/compliance value surfaced;
- buyer willingness to pay for auditability vs time savings;
- deployment and integration cost;
- recurring vs one-off workflow needs.

## Demo narrative

Use sanitized data only:

1. show three supplier offers;
2. show separated quotation/payment/vendor/technical evidence;
3. show numerical ranking;
4. show a policy gate changing automatic eligibility;
5. show the recommended policy-eligible supplier;
6. open the decision explanation;
7. verify the decision record/provenance;
8. show the human review path for a REVIEW case.

The demo should prove why the system is more trustworthy than a single hidden score.
